11 steps from parts to a working room.

Room 101 is split into four zones, each watched by one mmWave sensor:

  • A front-left · B front-right · C back-left · D back-right

Five circuits, each serving some zones. A shared circuit stays on until all its zones are empty:

  • Lights front row → A+B · Lights back row → C+D
  • Fans left → A+C · Fans right → B+D
  • Split AC (over IR) → all zones, with a 3-minute compressor lockout

Try it: in the Simulator, choose “Small group in A”. Only the front lights and left fans stay on.

  • ESP32 DevKit V1 (38-pin) ×1
  • HLK-LD2410C mmWave sensor ×4 (2 are enough for a first test)
  • 4-channel 5 V opto-isolated relay module with JD-VCC jumper ×1
  • 940 nm IR LED + 2N2222 + 1 kΩ and 68 Ω resistors (AC control)
  • TSOP1838 IR receiver (only for capturing the remote)
  • 100 kΩ resistors ×4 (sensor pull-downs)
  • 5 V 2 A adapter (USB is too weak for 4 relays)
  • 4 LEDs + 220 Ω resistors for bench testing; breadboard, jumpers, enclosure

Team estimate: about ₹3,000–3,500 per room for electronics.

  • Mount each LD2410C at 1–1.5 m on a wall (or on the ceiling), pointing into its own zone.
  • In the HLKRadarTool phone app (Bluetooth), set max moving and stationary distance gates (0.75 m per gate) so each sensor sees only its zone.
  • Moving people are detected farther than still ones, so tune both.
  • mmWave passes through thin wood, plastic and glass; don’t count on a partition.
  • Ceiling fans and curtains can trigger it: lower sensitivity on those gates.

In 3D, the green cones show each sensor’s tuned range. Use the “Range” button to show or hide them.

  • VCC → 5 V, GND → GND
  • OUT → GPIO 34 (A), 35 (B), 32 (C), 33 (D)
  • 100 kΩ from each OUT to GND so an unplugged sensor reads “empty”

OUT is 3.3 V logic, safe for the ESP32. GPIO 34/35 are input-only and have no internal pull-down, which is why the resistor matters.

  • IN1 → GPIO 25 (lights front) · IN2 → 26 (lights back)
  • IN3 → GPIO 27 (fans left) · IN4 → 13 (fans right)
  • Remove the JD-VCC jumper. Power JD-VCC from the 5 V adapter, and VCC from the ESP32’s 3.3 V pin, so coils are isolated from logic.
  • Join the ESP32 and adapter grounds.

GPIO 14 is avoided because it pulses at boot and would click a relay. Most modules switch ON with a LOW input (RELAY_ACTIVE_LOW = true).

Bench test first: put LEDs + 220 Ω on the relay pins and set RELAY_ACTIVE_LOW = false.

GPIO 4 ─ 1 kΩ ─ 2N2222 base
2N2222 emitter ─ GND
2N2222 collector ─ IR LED cathode
IR LED anode ─ 68 Ω ─ 5 V

Point the LED at the AC’s receiver window, within about 5 m with a clear line of sight. In 3D, the violet beam goes from the box to the AC.

Never switch an AC through a relay or extension board. SmartZone uses IR and sends the AC’s full state, never a power toggle.
  1. Wire the TSOP1838: VS → 3.3 V, GND → GND, OUT → GPIO 15.
  2. Arduino IDE: File → Examples → IRremoteESP8266 → IRrecvDumpV2, set kRecvPin = 15, upload.
  3. Press the remote’s power button at the receiver. The Serial Monitor prints e.g. Protocol : DAIKIN.
  4. Put it in config.h:
static const decode_type_t AC_PROTOCOL = decode_type_t::DAIKIN;

If it shows UNKNOWN, the library doesn’t support that AC and the firmware has no raw-code mode. Demo lights and fans and show AC as roadmap.

Open http://localhost:8000 for the dashboard and /docs for the API. Optional env vars: SMARTZONE_DEVICE_KEY, SMARTZONE_ADMIN_KEY, SMARTZONE_DB, SMARTZONE_ROOMS.

The backend rejects reports whose zone or circuit count doesn’t match rooms.json, so a mismatch shows up as a 422 in the Serial Monitor.

Before any appliance is connected, with LEDs as loads:

  • Stand in each zone in turn. Only that zone’s circuits should switch on (A → front lights + left fans + AC).
  • Sit very still for a minute: the zone must stay on (that’s what mmWave is for).
  • Leave: circuits go off after the off-delay. Shared circuits stay on while any of their zones is occupied.
  • Empty the room, then re-enter within 3 minutes: the AC must wait out its lockout.

Rehearse all four in the Simulator tab first; watch the AC lockout countdown.

Mains can kill. Permanent installation must be done by a licensed electrician and approved by the facility owner.
  • Only the live wire goes through the relay’s COM/NO. Neutral and earth go straight through.
  • Enclosed box with cable glands and strain relief; have it checked before switching on.
  • 10 A relays are fine for lights and fans, but stay well under the rating (inrush, inductive loads).
  • Central AC units: a compressor-rated contactor installed by an electrician, with a minimum off-time.
