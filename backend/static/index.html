Occupancy-based appliance control

Power only where
people are.

SmartZone splits a room into zones and switches lights, fans and AC on only in the zones where people are sitting. When a zone empties, it waits a few minutes, then switches it off.

The problem

Rooms are lit and cooled for people who aren't there

In classrooms, libraries, labs and offices, lights, fans and ACs run across the whole room even when people sit in only one part of it. The switches don't let anyone do better.

1 switch

Switches cover whole rooms

A light row or fan group runs from one switch, from the front of the room to the back. You can't light just the corner where people sit.

Part-full

Partial occupancy is normal

A small class in a big hall, a library at opening time, an office on a hybrid-work day. A half-empty room is the usual case, not the exception.

Nobody

Nobody walks over to switch off

People don't cross a room to turn off the empty side, and motion sensors switch off on people reading or typing, so staff disable them.

Use cases

Where SmartZone fits

Any large room where people sit in groups and the lighting, fans or cooling cover more space than the people use.

Classrooms and lecture halls

Schools, colleges, coaching centres
The situation
An elective with a handful of students in a large room, all sitting near the front.
What SmartZone does
Front-row lights and the fans over the occupied seats run. The back rows stay off. The AC runs while anyone is in the room.

Libraries and reading halls

College and public libraries, study rooms
The situation
A few readers spread across the hall in the early morning or late evening, sitting still for an hour at a time.
What SmartZone does
Radar keeps a zone on for a still reader, where motion sensors would switch it off. Empty reading bays switch off after the delay.

Offices and labs

Open-plan offices, computer labs, workshops
The situation
On hybrid-work days half the desks are empty. Lab benches are used in shifts.
What SmartZone does
Desk bays with nobody in them switch off. Circuits that must stay on, such as equipment, are set to "On" from the dashboard.

Auditoriums and seminar halls

Event halls, conference rooms
The situation
A small talk in a big hall, or a rehearsal with only the stage and front rows in use.
What SmartZone does
Zones map to seating blocks. The AC lockout protects compressors when people come and go.
How it works

From someone sitting down to a logged kilowatt-hour

  1. Detect

    A 24 GHz mmWave radar in each zone detects people, including the small movement of someone sitting still.

  2. Decide

    The ESP32 marks the zone active and works out which circuits serve it. A shared circuit stays on while any of its zones is in use.

  3. Switch

    Relays switch lights and fans. The AC gets its own remote's full-state IR command; no relay ever cuts its compressor.

  4. Hold

    When a zone empties, a 3-minute off-delay runs (adjustable 30 s – 30 min), so nothing flickers when someone steps out.

  5. Log

    The device reports every change and every 30 s. The dashboard shows energy used against what the same room would use with ordinary switches.

Architecture

Decisions are made in the room. The server keeps the records.

If Wi-Fi or the server goes down, switching carries on. The server stores reports, sends settings back, and works out energy figures.

Engineering choices

Why it is built this way

mmWave radar, not PIR

PIR sensors see only motion, so they switch the lights off on someone reading. 24 GHz radar picks up breathing-level movement and keeps the zone on.

The shared-circuit rule

Real rooms have light rows and fans that span several zones. A circuit switches off only when every zone it serves is empty, so SmartZone works on existing wiring.

AC by IR, with a 3-minute lockout

Cutting power to a compressor damages ACs. SmartZone sends the AC's full state (power, mode, 24 °C) over IR and keeps it off for at least 3 minutes after switching off.

Edge-first and offline-safe

Sensing and switching never wait for the network. Reporting runs as a separate task, and missed reports are covered by the next 30-second snapshot.

A fair savings baseline

Savings are measured against ordinary switches used perfectly (everything off the moment the room empties), not against "on all day". Gaps in data are left out, not guessed.

No cameras

Radar reports presence only. No images, no identities, nothing to leak.
